ABOUT

Studying how tumours interact with the immune system

Dr. Reza Mirzaei is an Assistant Professor in the Department of Oncology at the University of Alberta, with a cross-appointment in the Department of Medical Microbiology & Immunology, and a Research Scientist at the Cross Cancer Institute.

He established his independent research program at the University of Alberta in 2024. His laboratory investigates the mechanisms underlying tumour progression, tumour–immune interactions, and resistance to cancer immunotherapy.

TRAINING

Research experience across cancer and immunology

Dr. Mirzaei received his MSc and PhD training in Immunology, followed by postdoctoral training at the University of Calgary, where he studied the brain tumour microenvironment and the complex interactions between cancer cells and immune cells.

He subsequently continued his research career at the Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai and Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center in New York. His work focused on tumour immunology, tumour–microenvironment interactions, and mechanisms that regulate responses to cancer immunotherapy.

RESEARCH PROGRAM

Connecting cancer biology with immune responses

His current research program integrates cancer immunology, functional genomics, CRISPR-based screening, and single-cell and spatial technologies to uncover mechanisms that control tumour progression and therapeutic response.

The laboratory has a particular interest in glioblastoma and colorectal cancer, using complementary experimental and computational approaches to study tumour heterogeneity and the immune microenvironment.

SCIENTIFIC FOCUS

How cancer reshapes its surroundings

A major focus of the lab is understanding how cancer cells communicate with and reshape the immune microenvironment. The team investigates how myeloid cells, including tumour-associated macrophages and microglia, interact with diverse cancer cell populations.

These interactions can influence anti-tumour immunity, tumour evolution, and resistance to immunotherapy.

LONG-TERM GOAL

Finding new paths toward more effective treatments

By combining mechanistic studies with functional genomics and high-resolution single-cell and spatial profiling, the research aims to identify tumour-intrinsic and immune-regulatory pathways that can be therapeutically targeted.

The long-term goal is to translate these discoveries into new therapeutic targets, biomarkers, and treatment strategies that improve the effectiveness and durability of cancer immunotherapy.
